## Q1. CDMO: 삼성바이오로직스 (207940), SK바이오사이언스 (302440†), 에스티팜 (237690†), and US policy (BIOSECURE, Section 232 tariffs)

### Takeaway
삼성바이오로직스 has the clearest durable moat among Korean healthcare companies. It runs about 845,000 L of capacity (Songdo plus Rockville, Maryland), Plants 1–4 run full, cumulative orders reached $22.3B, and Q3 2026 revenue rose 19%. Two US policy changes help its position: the BIOSECURE Act (law since Dec 2025, though enforcement is slow and WuXi Biologics is not yet designated) and a 15% cap on Section 232 pharma tariffs for Korea. 에스티팜 is a smaller niche player, an oligonucleotide CDMO with a $270M backlog. SK바이오사이언스's CDMO earnings come mostly from its German subsidiary IDT Biologika, and the parent company still reports operating losses.

**US BIOSECURE Act**
- The Act was signed into law on 2025-12-18 as part of the FY2026 NDAA. Unlike the 2024 bill, it does not name WuXi AppTec, WuXi Biologics, BGI, MGI, or Complete Genomics. A company becomes a "biotechnology company of concern" only through the DoD 1260H list or an OMB list, and none of the WuXi entities had been designated at enactment. — [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/01/biosecure-act-enacted); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/biosecure-act-becomes-law-limiting-grants-with-biotechnology-companies-of-concern)
- FDA Law Blog reports that DoD's updated 1260H list (published 2026-06-08) newly added WuXi AppTec. Only one source reported this. TechTimes (Nov 2026) says WuXi Biologics has not been separately designated. — [FDA Law Blog](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2026/06/wuxi-apptecs-1260h-listing-brings-the-biosecure-act-back-to-center-stage/); [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/329999/20261103/samsung-biologics-logs-323m-contract-biosecure-act-redirection-enters-procurement-data.htm)

### Inferences
- Samsung Biologics' moat is a combination of (1) scale: the largest single-company biologics capacity, which lowers cost per liter; (2) switching costs: once a drug's process is validated and approved at a specific plant, moving it requires a new tech transfer and regulatory filings, so clients tend to stay for the product's life; (3) a regulatory track record and speed (repeat clients and multi-year contracts through 2031). The 845,000 L figure plus a 1.385M L roadmap means competitors would need years and billions to match it.
- BIOSECURE is a tailwind, not yet a step-change. The law no longer names WuXi Biologics, and enforcement is likely 2028 or later. The near-term effect is "de-risking" orders: big pharma qualifying a non-Chinese second source. Q3 2026 OP grew only 1% despite 19% revenue growth, because new-plant start-up costs and the strike diluted margins.
- The tariff picture is broadly favorable for Korean CDMOs. The 15% cap for Korea is lower than the 100% for non-deal countries, and the Rockville acquisition gives Samsung a US-based manufacturing option. A CDMO's exposure depends on whether its clients' products are patented, branded drugs; most of Samsung's are, so the 15% cap matters more than the generic exemption.
- 에스티팜 has a narrower but real niche (oligonucleotide API at commercial scale is supply-constrained globally). Its moat is weaker than Samsung's because it is smaller, margins are falling, and revenue is concentrated in a few commercial products.
- SK바이오사이언스's improvement comes from an acquired German asset. Group CDMO profitability is not yet proven, and the moat is weak to moderate.

### Inferences
- Celltrion's moat: (1) it was first to market with antibody biosimilars and has sold in Europe for about 10 years, with its own direct-sales organization in Europe and the US; (2) vertical integration (in-house development, manufacturing, and sales) and high utilization give it cost leadership; (3) it has moved into differentiated products: Zymfentra is approved in the US as a new drug and has patent protection, unlike standard biosimilars; (4) the US Branchburg plant hedges against tariffs and BIOSECURE and adds CMO revenue.
- The biosimilar moat is moderate rather than strong. Each molecule faces 5–10 competitors (Sandoz, Amgen, Alvotech, Samsung Bioepis, etc.), and prices keep falling. Celltrion has offset that through portfolio breadth and first-wave launches. The 33% OP margin is evidence of real scale advantages, but the biosimilar tariff exemption could be revisited around April 2027.
- Samsung Bioepis is a credible number-2 with a strong pipeline and Samsung Biologics' manufacturing behind it. Growth (about +6% in H1) lags Celltrion, and the holding-company structure has weighed on shareholder value since the split.

### Inferences
- In plain terms, Korea's durable healthcare advantages fall into three groups:
  1. Manufacturing excellence at scale (Samsung Biologics, Celltrion). Korea's chaebol-style capital allocation built the world's largest biologics capacity quickly, and validated processes keep clients locked in.
  2. Fast-follower, cost-efficient biologics (biosimilars, SC versions).
  3. Consumer-medical crossover in aesthetics, where a huge, competitive domestic clinic market works as an R&D and marketing testbed and Korean products win abroad on price-performance.
- Novel-drug discovery moats are mostly unproven. Platform deals bring validation and upfront cash, but only Alteogen has a commercial royalty stream so far, and that stream is small per unit and legally contested.
- US policy in 2025–2026 (BIOSECURE, a 15% Korea tariff cap, a temporary biosimilar exemption) tilts competition toward Korean manufacturers relative to China. The actual impact is gradual and partly offset by US-onshoring pressure, which is why Samsung (Rockville) and Celltrion (Branchburg) both bought US plants.
