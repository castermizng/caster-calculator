## Key Question 1: Which Korea-listed materials/industrial/auto companies still have durable niche global moats? (company-by-company check)

### Takeaway
The strongest verified moats are niche process or scale businesses that China has not copied cheaply:
- 고려아연's multi-metal smelting, now with a US government-backed US smelter.
- HS효성첨단소재's PET tire cord (about 50% global share).
- 효성티앤씨's spandex leadership. Its pricing power returned in 2026 as Chinese rivals cut back.
- 현대차/기아's hybrid lineup. Kia passed a 10% share of the US hybrid market in Q3 2026.
- 두산밥캣's compact equipment franchise in North America.

Moats that rest on policy rather than product are real but fragile:
- 한화솔루션/큐셀's US solar plant depends on the US AMPC tax credit (45X, created under the IRA) and on tariff barriers.
- SKC/Absolics' glass substrate is still pre-revenue; mass production has slipped to 2027.
- Robot companies (두산로보틱스, 레인보우로보틱스) have no proven moat yet.

### Inferences
- **Moat strength ranking (my judgment from the evidence above):**
  1. **Strong / durable:**
     - 고려아연: multi-metal smelting, critical minerals, and a US government equity partner.
     - HS효성첨단소재: PET tire cord at about 50% global share, with only 3–4 producers outside China.
     - 효성티앤씨: spandex scale leader, now gaining as Chinese rivals cut back.
  2. **Strong but cyclical or policy-exposed:**
     - 현대차/기아: hybrid strength, but margin guidance was cut, and tariffs plus Middle East costs are hitting.
     - 두산밥캣: North American compact equipment brand, but Q3 profit fell sharply from Q2.
     - 한국타이어: premium tires with margins around 17%.
  3. **Policy moat:** 한화솔루션/큐셀. Profitability depends on US AMPC credits and tariffs; Q3 renewable margin is only about 4.9% (105.1/2,163.2).
  4. **Unproven:** SKC glass substrate, now targeted for 2027 with no confirmed customer mass-production order, and the robot companies (valuation running ahead of earnings; one has a legal overhang).
- **Korea Zinc governance is itself a moat risk.** The US Crucible JV holds about 10% and won the most AGM votes, which makes the US effectively a kingmaker. That likely makes a hostile MBK takeover harder, but it also dilutes other shareholders and keeps the fight in court. The gap between Choi's "45.5%" (January) and "39%" (September) is probably a counting difference, for example whether the JV's stake is counted as friendly. That is my inference and is not confirmed.
- **Background from general knowledge (pre-2026, not re-verified here):** Korea Zinc has long been described as the world's largest zinc smelter and a producer of minor metals such as indium, bismuth, antimony and (planned) germanium. Hyundai-Kia has been the world's #3 auto group by volume since about 2022. Treat both as context, not as verified 2026 facts.
- **Hyundai/Kia's moat has moved from price to product (hybrids).** Hybrids are winning US share while the 15% tariff (versus 0% under the old KORUS FTA) squeezes margins. The Q3 profit gains rest largely on a lower tariff base and FX, not on organic margin expansion.

## Key Question 2: Which once-strong Korean industries are losing their moat (mainly to China), and what edge is left?

### Takeaway
Losing ground, roughly in order of severity:
- **Petrochemicals (worst):** Chinese overcapacity forced a government-led cut of about 3.4–3.7Mt of naphtha cracker (NCC) capacity in 2026.
- **EV batteries:** China held about 73% of global EV battery use in Jan–Sep 2026, and LG에너지솔루션's share fell to 8.2%.
- **Steel:** margins are thin, though China's exports eased somewhat.
- **Display:** BOE leads total smartphone panels; Samsung still leads smartphone OLED.

What is left:
- Batteries have pivoted to US ESS (energy storage). It made LGES and Samsung SDI profitable in Q3 2026.
- Samsung Display keeps the high end (iPhone, foldables). LG Display is now 67% OLED.
- POSCO's profit recovered, helped by Chinese price hikes and its own furnace maintenance.

## Key Question 3: Korean market context (late 2026): KOSPI level and performance, Korea discount, governance reforms, MSCI, foreign flows, and how these affect moat companies

### Takeaway
KOSPI ran from 2,399 (end-2024) to 4,214 (end-2025, +75.6%) to a 9,385.59 intraday peak (19 June 2026). It then crashed. On 6 November 2026 it fell below 5,000 and hit its 11th circuit breaker of the year, leaving it down about 47% from the peak but still about 17% above end-2025.

The rally and the crash were both driven mainly by memory/AI chips. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix make up more than half of KOSPI's market cap, and foreigners sold heavily.

Governance reforms are now law:
- Directors' duty to shareholders and the 3% rule: 1st Commercial Act amendment, 2025.
- Mandatory cumulative voting and at least two separately elected audit-committee members for firms with assets of 2tn won or more: 2nd amendment, passed August 2025, reportedly in force 10 September 2026.
- Mandatory cancellation of treasury shares within one year: 3rd amendment, passed 25 February 2026, in force 6 March 2026.

The index P/B multiple has roughly doubled. Even so, about 60% of listed firms still trade below book value, and MSCI declined to put Korea on its developed-market watch list in June 2026.

### Inferences
- **2026 performance in numbers (my calculations from cited levels):**
  - Year to date to the 6 November intraday low (4,924.61 vs. 4,214.17): about +16.9%.
  - From the 19 June intraday peak (9,385.59): about −47.5%.
  - The 6 November closing level was not available.
  - For moat companies the market backdrop matters more than their own fundamentals right now. Index-level forced selling (leverage unwinds, foreign outflows, program-trading sidecars and circuit breakers) hits all large caps regardless of moat.
- **"Korea discount" partly closed, then reopened.** Index P/B rose from 1.35x to 2.4x mostly because of a few chip and large-cap names. Most firms, including steel, petrochemical and auto-parts names, stayed below book. After the crash the index P/B is likely back near 1.3–1.5x. That is an estimate scaled from price moves, not a sourced figure.
- **How the reforms touch the companies in Key Questions 1–2:**
  - **Cumulative voting and separate audit-committee elections** (assets of 2tn won or more): these directly empower activists and rival shareholders. 고려아연 already shows it: Crucible JV's nominee topped the cumulative vote, and the audit-committee expansion failed with 53.89%. Expect more contested 2027 AGMs at chaebol affiliates, including 현대차그룹, 한화, 효성 and 롯데.
  - **Treasury-share cancellation:** firms holding large treasury stakes must cancel them or win shareholder approval, which removes a defensive tool. This matters for holding-company structures (e.g., HS효성/효성 group firms, 롯데) and for control fights such as 고려아연's.
  - **Dividend tax cut:** it favors cash-rich, high-payout moat companies (현대차 at 2,500 won quarterly, 기아, 두산밥캣 with a 1,600 won minimum annual dividend). The size of the benefit depends on the final top rate, which is unconfirmed.
  - **MSCI:** no upgrade before 2028, so the structural passive-inflow catalyst is delayed. Foreign selling in 2026 adds liquidity risk for mid-cap moat names (HS효성첨단소재, 효성티앤씨).
- **Concentration risk:** with Samsung plus SK Hynix above 50% of KOSPI market cap, "Korean market" moves are largely memory-cycle moves. The materials, industrial and auto moat companies are a diversification within Korea, but they still suffer in index-wide sell-offs.
