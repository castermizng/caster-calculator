## 1. Korean shipbuilders (HD한국조선해양, HD현대중공업, HD현대미포, 한화오션, 삼성중공업): global share, backlog, prices, China and Japan

### Takeaway
Korea is no longer the volume leader. China took about 77% of global orders by CGT in Jan–Oct 2026 versus about 15% for Korea. Korea's moat is now narrow and specific: large LNG carriers (still a majority share but slipping toward China), FLNG/offshore, and high-spec gas carriers. It rests on its delivery record, its quality and the confidence of customers paying for a premium. 2026 earnings are at record levels because high-priced 2022–24 orders are now being booked as revenue. The forward risk: China is winning LNG carriers at lower prices, LNG newbuild prices are slipping, and brokers are cutting targets.

### Inferences
- **The moat is real but narrowing.** Korea's durable edge is concentrated in large membrane LNG carriers, FLNG/offshore gas units, and specialized gas carriers (LPG, ethane, ammonia). Here owners pay for track record, low boil-off performance, on-time delivery and financing comfort. In commodity segments (bulkers, most container ships, tankers), China's cost and capacity advantage dominates, and Korea's share there is cyclical, not structural.
- **Ranking within Korea, by moat quality:** HD한국조선해양/HD현대중공업 is the broadest (largest scale; in-house engines via the engine and machinery division at a 24% margin; naval; widest ship mix; highest margin at 17.5%). 삼성중공업 has the deepest niche moat in FLNG/offshore gas, where few yards worldwide compete. 한화오션 has strong LNG and naval capability plus the most direct US exposure (Philly), but more execution volatility (naval loss, the Canada loss).
- **The earnings visibility is high, but it is a lagging indicator.** 2026–27 profits reflect 2022–24 pricing. With LNG prices easing ($248M → $245M) and Chinese bids around $225M, margins on new 2026 orders are likely below the current peak. This is an inference: no source quantified new-order margins.
- **Backlog length:** dock saturation through 2029 implies about 3+ years of visibility for the big yards. That protects near-term pricing even as China gains share.

### Gaps
- No primary Clarksons release was accessible. LNG carrier share for 2026 YTD conflicts across sources (Korea 53.7% vs 62% vs China 57.8%). There are no reliable 2026 figures for Korea's share in ammonia/methanol dual-fuel ships, VLCCs or container ships.
- Samsung Heavy's exact Q3 2026 backlog in won (implied: about 54.8조원 = 50.5968 × 1.084, a calculation, not reported) and its 2026 order-target achievement rate were not found. HD한국조선해양's consolidated 2025 full-year results and total backlog were not confirmed (search returned conflicting numbers).
- No October 2026 Clarksons Newbuilding Price Index value was found.
- The details and duration of the HD현대중공업 work stoppage (mentioned by KB) were not confirmed.

## 2. US–Korea shipbuilding cooperation (MASGA), US Navy MRO, Hanwha Philly Shipyard: status in 2026

### Takeaway
MASGA ("Make American Shipbuilding Great Again", the $150B Korean pledge inside the $350B US–Korea investment deal) has produced real but small results by Nov 2026: Hanwha's first US Navy newbuild contract ($269M), a run of 7th Fleet auxiliary-ship MRO jobs, and a joint research center. The $150B fund itself is stalled over financing. It adds a geopolitical layer to the moat (a "trusted ally" yard versus China) more than near-term earnings.

### Inferences
- MASGA strengthens the moat mainly through **geopolitical exclusivity**: as the US restricts Chinese-built ships and rebuilds naval capacity, Korean yards (and Japan) are the only allied yards with the scale and skill. That is a barrier China cannot cross, whatever its prices.
- In money terms it is still small. One $269M ship (work from 2028) and a few MRO jobs, each likely tens of millions of dollars, are immaterial next to $36–61B backlogs. The upside is optional: a nuclear submarine program, US Navy auxiliary or combat-ship construction, or a change to US law allowing overseas-built warships. It is not yet in the numbers.
- 한화오션 has the most direct US exposure (and the most direct China-retaliation exposure). HD현대 is spreading its MRO capability and US partnerships. 삼성중공업 is a latecomer.

## 3. Shipbuilding equipment and parts: which have true moats?

### Takeaway
The strongest equipment moats are where the global supplier pool is tiny and qualification is long. The best evidence is for **LNG cargo-hold insulation (동성화인텍, 한국카본)**, which sits on the GTT membrane licensing chain, and **marine engine licensees and aftermarket (HD현대마린솔루션, HD현대중공업's engine division, 한화엔진, HD현대마린엔진)**. Pipe fittings (성광벤드, 태광) and LPG tank fabricators (세진중공업) are more cyclical, and evidence of a moat is weak.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **LNG insulation (동성화인텍, 한국카본): true but derivative moat.** Very few makers worldwide are qualified for GTT membrane insulation panels and super-low-temperature materials, and 동성화인텍 claims about 94% of one new GTT system's confirmed hulls. Their moat depends on LNG carrier volume and on GTT's licensing. If China builds more LNG carriers with local insulation suppliers, their addressable share shrinks. Supplying Chinese yards is both a hedge and a margin dilutor.
- **Engines and aftermarket: the strongest equipment moat.** The two-stroke engine market runs on licenses (WinGD/MAN designs built by a few licensees). HD현대 (HHI engine division plus HD현대마린엔진) and 한화엔진 are among the world's largest licensees. Dual-fuel (LNG/methanol/ammonia) engines raise complexity and value per engine. HD현대마린솔루션 has an installed-base moat: decades of HD현대-built ships and engines need OEM parts and service, which explains its seven straight record aftermarket quarters. It is also the least cyclical in the group, because aftermarket revenue follows the existing fleet, not new orders. This is inference from the cited figures plus general industry structure.
- **Pipe fittings (성광벤드, 태광) and 세진중공업: weak or cyclical moat.** Their demand is driven by plant and LNG-terminal capex and ship volume. The Q1 2026 revenue decline shows that cyclicality. They have qualification-based customer stickiness, but no evidence was found of pricing power or global share leadership.

## 4. Defense (한화에어로스페이스, 현대로템, LIG D&A (formerly LIG넥스원), KAI, 한화시스템, 풍산): exports, backlog, why Korea wins, risks

### Takeaway
Korea's defense moat is the ability to **deliver NATO-compatible, combat-proven-class land systems and missiles quickly, from warm mass-production lines, at a lower price** than Western rivals. Its export base is now broad (Poland, Romania, Finland, Spain, Croatia, Egypt, Australia, UAE/Saudi/Iraq, Malaysia, Philippines). Backlogs are huge, about 4 years of work across the sector. But Q3 2026 showed lumpy, delivery-timing-driven earnings. Follow-on European deals are slowing as buyers demand localization, and stocks have corrected sharply (현대로템 down 67% from its May 2026 peak).

- **Why Korea wins:** (1) **Delivery speed from warm lines.** Korea's own large standing army keeps K9, K2 and Chunmoo lines running, so exports ship in months to a few years, while Western backlogs run 5+ years. (2) **NATO compatibility.** 155mm/52-cal artillery and 120mm tank gun standards fit European logistics. (3) **Price-performance:** cheaper than German or US equivalents with comparable specs. (4) **Willingness to localize and transfer technology** (Krab chassis, Romania K9 plant, K2PL assembly in Poland). (5) **Family-of-systems bundles** (K9+K10+ammunition; Cheongung + 한화시스템 radar; FA-50 + training). The first two are the hardest for rivals to copy quickly.
- **Moat ranking (inference):** 한화에어로스페이스 has the widest moat. K9 holds the largest global share of tracked self-propelled howitzers (prior knowledge, not re-verified in 2026), it has many customers and a large, diversified backlog. Air defense demand is structural after the Ukraine and Iran conflicts, but margins are under R&D pressure. 현대로템 has a strong product but customer concentration (Poland). Its next leg depends on the unsigned 3rd K2 contract and on Peru, Romania and Iraq. KAI has a light-fighter and trainer niche (FA-50) plus KF-21 optionality, but KF-21 exports are still unproven. 한화시스템 is a high-quality component supplier riding sister-company and LIG platforms. 풍산's ammunition earnings are strong but its moat is less clear, being a commodity-like product with a copper-price overlay.
- **Earnings pattern:** defense results are lumpy, driven by delivery schedules. Q3 2026 misses at 한화에어로 (-46%) and LIG (-25%) were mainly timing and FX, not lost business. Backlogs remain about 4+ years of revenue.
- **Key risks:** localization demands eroding margins and share of value; EU "buy European" preferences (the Romania IFV loss to Rheinmetall); Poland concentration and its budget and financing; a possible Ukraine ceasefire reducing European urgency; FX (a stronger KRW cut Q3 profits); export-financing limits (Korea's state export-credit caps, a gap in this research); Middle East geopolitics.
